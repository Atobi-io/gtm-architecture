Overview

Engine that can run lead generation campaigns

The goal of the engine is to create leads that are qualified enough to be handed over to a sales channel (phone, meeting). It creates those leads by communicating in the market either to many through broad campaigns or to one through one-to-one outreach to relevant stakeholders.

Important principles
1 · What runs in the engine: campaigns and routines

The engine’s unit of work is defined entirely by its brief; a company runs many at once. It comes in exactly two mutually exclusive kinds, split by the brief’s existing type field — no new classifier.

A campaign is timebound: a start, an end and a fixed campaign plan (an arc; three contracts incl. project-plan.md) — e.g. Brand Ambassadors. A routine is ongoing: no end date, running on a continuous rhythm or on signals (a cadence; two contracts) — post-engage, saved-posts, industry-trending, new-follower welcome, voice-learn. Beyond that the mechanics are identical: same contract chain, same workspace shape, same queue, same conductor; presented as two hub pages over one shared Notion DB.

Touches in either kind can be scheduled (planned on a calendar), triggered (fired by a signal — a reply, an engagement threshold), or a mix of both; a campaign’s triggers are described in its campaign plan. Send-time is plan-owned too: the campaign-plan’s Send-tid grammar decides when a channel’s touches go out; Stage 3 stamps it as an optional per-touch scheduled_time in touch.yaml, and the conductor’s delivery step gates on it.

2 · Campaign-specific vs generic

Everything specific to a campaign lives in its brief; everything else in the engine is generic and shared across all campaigns — Foundation (subject + industry knowledge), content production, channel execution, monitoring/scoring.

3 · The brief is the contract

The engine reads a brief and orchestrates the generic capabilities end to end for that campaign. The brief is the contract; the rest is tooling.

4 · Accounts live in Foundation

Accounts belong to industries (part of industry research). The aggregate account lists live in Foundation as a registry, so any campaign can target the whole list or a slice — CRM-like (the list may ultimately belong in an actual CRM).

5 · Content and presentation are separate

Content production engineers the substance: it makes every asset a campaign needs complete and channel-aware, but it does not write the finished channel format. Channel execution is the presentation layer: it takes those assets and assembles & delivers them in each channel’s final form.

6 · Channel executors share one anatomy

Every channel executor (/gtm-channel-{x}) is the same four-step shape, only the channel format varies. Adding a channel is stamping the shared template — fill in the format spec and the channel fields, nothing else; a fix to the anatomy propagates to every future stamp via the template.

1 Input ← Stage 2 content artefact (voice-gated + humanized) + campaign context 2 Constraints ← format spec in the executor itself 3 Assemble ← Stage 2 body verbatim (voice lives in Stage 2) 4 Output → pending queue → review
7 · Knowledge and knobs live apart

Every piece of information has one home, decided by a single question: is it a truth or a dial? Durable truths about the Subject and its audience live in Foundation; truths that hold for only one campaign live in that campaign’s brief. Operational dials you tune — scoring weights, bindings, enforce-rules, queue presentation, campaign-manager knobs — live in settings and carry no knowledge. Campaign choices — cadence, channel mix, budgets — are brief truths, never global settings; per-channel format limits live in the channel executor itself. Bookkeeping about the build itself (open decisions, what’s pending) lives in the operating layer (ask.md).

A rule and the knowledge it acts on are split: “enforce no banned words” is a dial (settings); the list of banned words is voice knowledge (Foundation). The scoring weights are dials; the buyer titles are ideal-customer knowledge.

8 · Delivery is automatic — gated by approval, or by an explicit per-channel auto-policy

A deliverable touch auto-sends via Unipile through the conductor’s delivery step (Step 4b), using the send scripts in gtm-shared/unipile/. Seven channels: personal post · company post · connect · DM · comment · email · physical direct mail. Direct mail is assembled by the engine but always delivered by a human — print and post is a manual act, so the channel has no delivery block at all. Gating is deterministic and fail-closed: settings > delivery decides per channel which operators auto-send (absent section = everything stays manual); company posts carry the organization id; per-run pacing caps protect the accounts.

Approval is not the only trigger. The small touches — comments and connects — carry review: auto in settings > delivery and send without operator approval, each with a kill-switch (remove the line and everything on the channel needs the checkbox again, including auto-stamped items already queued), a reject option and a pre-approved new-version path in the queue. Posts, company posts, DMs and email remain approval-gated (“Jeg godkender indholdet”). The engine never writes approved: true itself — a human approval or the explicit auto-policy is the only path to a send, and every other gate (voice, humanizer, suppression, pacing, portfolio caps) is unchanged on auto channels. Only the engine_owner may change the auto-policy.

Wrong-target gate: every Unipile send verifies author/recipient identity against the source object before it fires — URNs are taken only from the post object, never reconstructed. Idempotent: a touch that already carries a Unipile id is never re-sent. HEIC images convert on the fly to a deliverable jpg in engine scratch (_temp/). Reads follow the same discipline: Unipile-first read policy — wherever the adapter endpoint exists, skills read through it; Chrome is a documented, flagged fallback only, never primary, never silent.

Cross-stage · the conductor
Runs the campaigns and routines forward, autonomously
gtm-campaign-manager— Reads every active campaign's and routine's contracts, decides what is due, drives Stage 2→3 into the panel on cadence, delivers approved touches (Step 4b, Unipile — principle 8), escalates what it cannot fix (control-panel/_campaign-management.md). Headless via per-job launchd plists: weekdays 09/13/17 + Sat/Sun 20:00.
Reaction layer: an event-driven queue-watcher (com.gist.gtm-queue-watch) reacts within minutes to operator edits in the panel — sync, correction-loop, delivery and ping runs only, never production; a 5-minute poll fallback covers Drive-synced edits from other machines, which do not fire WatchPaths.
Plan-reconciliation v2 (Step 2b): missed touches are re-slotted, retired or marked covered deterministically via gtm-shared/reconcile_touch.py — max 1 auto-reslot per touch, a date-only move preserves the operator's approval and never re-pings, retired is terminal.
Catch-up delivery: a skipped run never loses a post — the next successful run delivers every approved, still-fresh, past-due touch (max one personal post per operator per day when draining a backlog).
Portfolio enforcement: control-panel/_portfolio.md sets hard cross-campaign ceilings (max posts per operator per day, max outreach per person); the conductor enforces them at every run and escalates synergy overlaps to “Kræver din beslutning” + a coordinator mail ping.
Goal tracking: meetings are registered from the queue (“Registrér møde”, queue v4.3) into a meetings-ledger (tracking/engagement/meetings/); the conductor reports progress against the brief’s targets and escalates off-trajectory campaigns pro-rata. Paid gets the same loop: CSV-ingested ad metrics, a weekly loser-verdict and rotation proposals in the queue.
Routines (the second category of running work, principle 1; beside the conductor’s cadence): post-engage harvests engagement on the operators’ own posts daily at 08:30 (all connected operators, person-suppression gate); saved-posts reads each connected operator’s own Saved list 3×/weekday (08:45 · 12:45 · 16:45) and drafts a comment for every save; industry-trending turns the nightly trending scan into queued reactions; voice-learn runs weekly (Thursdays); new-follower welcome runs Tuesday + Thursday mornings (fail-closed).
Pause is two mechanisms: a campaign pauses via gtm-campaign-pause/-resume (production stops, produced-unapproved touches move to a Pauset view, approval resets on resume, the operator chooses push-or-drop for the arc; a pause expiry raises a decision, never a headless restart) — authority: campaign owner / coordinator. A routine pauses via a wrapper-skip on its job — authority: engine_owner only.
How it connects (post channel): campaign brief.md → gtm-creative writes near-final, voice-rendered content/{touch}/linkedin-{operator}.md (voice gate here) → gtm-channel-linkedin-post checks constraints, assembles verbatim, writes touch.yaml + a queue pointer → gtm-queue → operator approves (“Jeg godkender indholdet”) → delivered: auto-sent via Unipile for gated operators (Step 4b), posted manually otherwise. Connect / email / comment / DM executors are stamped from the same shared template. The whole path runs autonomously on cadence via gtm-campaign-manager. Company-page posts are a separate company-as-sender surface.
Roles

Roles and authority — campaigns and routines are free, the Foundation is guarded

The engine is operated by named operators — the roster IS foundation/company/persona-voices/ (one file = one operator) — with two authority roles layered on top. (Full convention: foundation/company/operating-conventions.md > Roller og myndighed; operator machine setup: OPERATORS.md in the skills repo.)

Operator

  • Daily surface is the control panel (the queue): reviews what the engine produced, approves it, comments when something needs to change. The comment is the lever that routes into the learning loop. Not decoration.
  • Campaigns and routines are free: any operator may create and activate them (/gtm-campaign-start; brief.md exists = active, no gate) and feed existing ones — e.g. push a newsletter into the trending routine.
  • How a fed-in input is handled is decided by the campaign brief, not the operator — the treatment (react-and-rewrite vs. light touch, which voice, which form) lives in the frame.

Engine owner

  • The guarded core: Foundation builds, settings/gtm-config.yaml and host machine + scheduling are engine_owner-only (settings > subject.engine_owner).
  • Works from chat, through the build skills — never by hand-editing the knowledge. Enforced mechanically: the Foundation write-protection hook blocks every other path (hard block).
  • Host + scheduling stay engine_owner: the cron-run loops behind the routines start, stop and pause only through the engine_owner (wrapper-skip on the job, never the campaign pause path). Any operator may still open a routine via /gtm-campaign-start — the guarded part is the machine, not the category.
  • If an operator asks for an engine_owner action, the engine refuses and refers to the engine_owner.

Campaign coordinator

  • Owns the portfolio, never the content: control-panel/_portfolio.md sets hard cross-campaign ceilings (max posts per day, max outreach per person), enforced by the conductor at every run.
  • Synergy overlaps between campaigns escalate to “Kræver din beslutning” + a coordinator mail ping; the coordinator judges on campaign collision. A new campaign acknowledges a flagged overlap via portfolio_ack in its brief.
  • Assigned in settings > subject.campaign_coordinator.
Campaigns and routines are free; what stays guarded is the Subject’s knowledge and the engine’s wiring. Anyone with a persona file can open and run a campaign. Only the engine_owner can change what the Subject knows (Foundation), how the engine is wired (settings), where it runs (host/scheduling) — including whether the routines’ scheduled jobs run. And how a fed-in input is handled is decided by the campaign brief, not the operator — the operator’s action stays dead-simple (“send this to campaign X”).
Prerequisite for “everything via the panel”: the engine generates each campaign’s and routine’s content on cadence into the panel by itself, without being asked each time. gtm-campaign-manager reads every active unit’s contracts on a schedule (per-job launchd plists: weekdays 09/13/17 + Sat/Sun 20:00), produces what is due through Stage 2→3 into the panel, delivers approved touches via Unipile (principle 8), and reports status + escalations to control-panel/_campaign-management.md — anything needing an admin decision surfaces there, not in chat. An event-driven queue-watcher (com.gist.gtm-queue-watch) reacts within minutes to operator edits in the panel — sync, correction-loop, delivery and ping runs only, never production.

2.1 · Folder structure

Recursive Agentic OS, scoped by the working directory

The folder you are in IS the Subject. Each Industry and each Account is a self-contained workspace. Stakeholders are leaf files, not folders.

Portable skills, Subject-specific choices. The same skill runs for any Subject with no change. Anything specific to one Subject (how operator messages are worded, each person's language, queue conventions) lives in the Subject's own files under foundation/company/, and skills point at them via gtm-config instead of hardcoding them. Same for working files: a skill reads pre-existing material read-only and writes all scratch to one dedicated _temp/ dir — never scattering files or cluttering the Subject's drive.
The signal layer (tracking/). A general staging room for what the engine learns while it runs — machine-written, not hand-edited. Signals mature here, then exit one of two ways: read directly at runtime (e.g. content-signals.yaml, a descriptive “what works” note the content skills reason over — no gate, self-correcting each cycle), or promoted into Foundation (e.g. lead scores into account lists) — always via a human-approved proposal delegated to the sanctioned build skill, never a direct write. The approval gate sits only on the path into Foundation; collection itself is automatic. Distinct from foundation/company/ (skill-write-only, cross-validated motor knowledge) precisely because it is machine-written and overwritten. Tenants: performance/ (organic + paid metrics) · engagement/ (pre-account engagement staging, captured leads, meetings-ledger) · notifications/ (ping ledgers); more added as built.
Materialise-on-demand, not required-at-rest. The drawn per-industry / per-account workspace shape (ask.md, _corpus/, marking/, stakeholders/, content/) is the shape a workspace takes when work happens there — it is not a structure every vertical must carry at rest. A vertical holding only vertical-card.yaml + accounts/ is complete, not a deviation. Today only sporting-goods carries the full research-body folders, and no audience-level ask.md exists anywhere. The thin register in settings (verticals: {id, priority}) says a vertical exists; the vertical-card carries its knowledge; the rest materialises when a campaign or research pass needs it.
The media library (bindings.media_library) — library-first. A Subject-neutral, Foundation-class image bank living outside the tree above (a Drive folder). The folder itself is human area — people drop images freely; its machine-readable catalogue {media_library}/INDEX.yaml is a motor artefact: skill-write-only, written with text-surgery via gtm-shared/media_bank.py, never a YAML round-trip. /gtm-media-index builds and refreshes the index. Bank before generation: gtm-creative looks the bank up before generating a new image, and a generated image is admitted to the bank only when the operator approves the post it belongs to.
The same engine-root also carries layers that are deliberately not the engine-runtime contract above — kept as separate folders because each serves a different reader, never nested into each other:
• Operator surface — control-panel/ (in the tree): the queue each operator approves and comments from. Their daily work-surface. (Skills write it; the operator reads it.)
• Instance operating / meta layer — ask.md · _admin/ · context/: read and written by the cross-cutting meta-skills (session-start, /ask, /note, /handover), never by the GTM runtime. _admin/ is the engagement's history (engagement-log, sessions, decisions, backlog, reports — “what happened”); context/ holds learnings.md, operator corrections skills read before running (“how to behave”) — the shared GIST learnings convention (~80 skills across all GIST work read this path, so it is NOT engine-local and stays put). Different concern, different reader — that is why _admin/, context/ and control-panel/ are siblings, not one inside another.
• Plumbing — _temp/ (disposable scratch, bindings.working_dir); cron/ (the ops-cron skill's job + log store: cron/jobs/ + cron/logs/, created only once a scheduled job exists — not part of the base instance shape, not an architecture element); and disposable build scaffolding _non-engine/.
None of these is engine-runtime knowledge; they are instance state, meta, and build-time scratch. Siblings outside the engine-root (engine-design blueprints, source material, archive) live one level up and are not Subject state at all.

The artefact file shapes

Every content and channel skill reads from here via CWD walk-up. Nothing about the Subject is hardcoded in any skill. The engine refuses to run if any required artefact is missing or incomplete.

brand-voice.md

  • Voice signature, anti-patterns, vocabulary in/out.
  • Sentence patterns, per-context calibrations.
  • Each claim grounded in a source quote.

positioning.md

  • Strategic positioning + competitive frame.
  • GTM angles per segment with proof-point binding.
  • Don't-say list.

ideal-customer.md

  • Firmographic ICP + buyer-role universe.
  • Role function (lead/support/exec/influencer/wildcard) + priority rank.
  • Segment classification rules.

proof-points/

  • One file per customer in proof-point shape.
  • Lookalike criteria, quantified results, attributed quotes.
  • Outreach angles, GTM-relevance tags.

product.md

  • Product / module taxonomy.
  • Capability → outcome mapping.
  • Pricing, deployment, proof per capability.

visual.md

  • Visual identity: fonts, colours, palette, logo.
  • Design judgment for branded assets.

persona-voices/

  • One file per operator voice (Jan, Kristian, …).
  • Read by the voice-driven content + channel skills.

operating-conventions.md

  • Instance conventions: operator ping format, per-operator language, queue conventions.
  • Decided by the Subject's people; skills bind to it via gtm-config, never hardcode it.

Extensible per Subject

  • Add regulatory-claims.md or partner-rules.md when relevant.
  • Engine functions without them, uses them when present.
  • _manifest.yaml locks versions + refresh triggers.
Not part of this contract: settings/gtm-config.yaml. The engine's knobs live in settings/, deliberately outside company — knobs, not knowledge. 13 keys: subject (bare identity + the two authority assignments engine_owner · campaign_coordinator) · cta_strategy · engagement_scoring · verticals (thin register, {id, priority} only — per-vertical knowledge lives in the vertical-card, §2.1) · bindings · runtime_constraints · performance · queue (incl. presentations: md always-on baseline + opt-in Notion Godkendelseskø per operator) · delivery (Unipile auto-send: per-channel operator gates, per-channel review: auto policy for comments + connects (principle 8), organization id for company posts, per-run pacing caps) · notifications (queue_ping per operator + coordinator_ping for portfolio escalations) · campaign_manager · reporting (management-report landing zone + Notion) · apollo (data-layer scripts + pacing caps; API key local, CSV fallback). No cadence and no channel register — cadence, channel mix and budgets are campaign-brief truths; format limits live in each channel executor; the operator roster IS persona-voices/ (one file = one operator).

Source of truth. The single reference for the engine architecture, carrying the engine overview (Concept) and §2.1–§2.2 (Technical). The industry- and account-reference contracts (§2.3–§2.4) are not part of this document; they live in the archived v3.2 until a rework lands. The domain-neutral distillation of this architecture lives in the companion doc engine-blueprint.html (same folder).

Konvention. Dette dokument beskriver NUTIDEN. Ingen datoer ud over topdatoen, ingen build-status eller badges, ingen før/efter- eller “siden”-formuleringer. Historik bor i _admin/decisions/ og engagement-loggen, kørsels-status i statusdokumentet (_admin/reports/), gaps i build-køen. En ændring af arkitekturen skrives ind som ren nutidsbeskrivelse.
