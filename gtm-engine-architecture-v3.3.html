Concept

The engine at a glance: the stages and how work flows across them.

Overview

Engine that can run lead generation campaigns

The goal of the engine is to create leads that are qualified enough to be handed over to a sales channel (phone, meeting). It creates those leads by communicating in the market either to many through broad campaigns or to one through one-to-one outreach to relevant stakeholders.

Important principles
1 · What runs in the engine: campaigns and routines

The engine’s unit of work is defined entirely by its brief; a company runs many at once. It comes in exactly two mutually exclusive kinds, split by the brief’s existing type field — no new classifier.

A campaign is timebound: a start, an end and a fixed campaign plan (an arc; three contracts incl. project-plan.md) — e.g. Brand Ambassadors. A routine is ongoing: no end date, running on a continuous rhythm or on signals (a cadence; two contracts) — post-engage, saved-posts, industry-trending, new-follower welcome, voice-learn. Beyond that the mechanics are identical: same contract chain, same workspace shape, same queue, same conductor; presented as two hub pages over one shared Notion DB.

2 · Campaign-specific vs generic

Everything specific to a campaign lives in its brief; everything else in the engine is generic and shared across all campaigns — Foundation (subject + industry knowledge), content production, channel execution, monitoring/scoring.

3 · The brief is the contract

The engine reads a brief and orchestrates the generic capabilities end to end for that campaign. The brief is the contract; the rest is tooling.

4 · Accounts live in Foundation

Accounts belong to industries (part of industry research). The aggregate account lists live in Foundation as a registry, so any campaign can target the whole list or a slice — CRM-like (the list may ultimately belong in an actual CRM).

5 · Content and presentation are separate

Content production engineers the substance: it makes every asset a campaign needs complete and channel-aware, but it does not write the finished channel format. Channel execution is the presentation layer: it takes those assets and assembles & delivers them in each channel’s final form.

6 · Channel executors share one anatomy

Every channel executor (/gtm-channel-{x}) is the same four-step shape, only the channel format varies. Adding a channel is stamping the shared template — fill in the format spec and the channel fields, nothing else; a fix to the anatomy propagates to every future stamp via the template.

7 · Knowledge and knobs live apart

Every piece of information has one home, decided by a single question: is it a truth or a dial? Durable truths about the Subject and its audience live in Foundation; truths that hold for only one campaign live in that campaign’s brief. Operational dials you tune — scoring weights, bindings, enforce-rules, queue presentation, campaign-manager knobs — live in settings and carry no knowledge. Campaign choices — cadence, channel mix, budgets — are brief truths, never global settings; per-channel format limits live in the channel executor itself. Bookkeeping about the build itself (open decisions, what’s pending) lives in the operating layer (ask.md).

A rule and the knowledge it acts on are split: “enforce no banned words” is a dial (settings); the list of banned words is voice knowledge (Foundation). The scoring weights are dials; the buyer titles are ideal-customer knowledge.

8 · Delivery is automatic — gated by approval, or by an explicit per-channel auto-policy

The engine sends deliverable touches itself; what opens the gate is either the operator’s approval or an explicit per-channel auto-policy declared by the engine owner. The engine never writes approved: true on its own authority — a human approval or the declared auto-policy is the only path to a send. Channels whose act is manual or whose platform is not plumbed for sending are assembled by the engine and delivered by a human; paid never launches on the engine’s authority.

Layers

The layer model

The engine’s knowledge is organised in five layers. Three belong to the engine and ship with every installation; two belong to the company and never leave it.

LayerWhat it isWhy do we have it?Engine or company?Why does it matter?
SkillsThe ability to perform a taskThe work must be done the same way every time, regardless of who pushes the buttonEngineSkills do all the work. The better they are, the better everything the engine delivers
DoctrinesThe engine’s convictions about good craft in a disciplineCraft rules would otherwise sit scattered across skills and briefs, where they drift apart and cannot be updated in one placeEngineEnsures consistent quality. Collects learning in one place
Playbook libraryA collection of proven templates for how to build good campaigns and routinesThe engine must be able to help the customer build good campaigns, and that requires knowing what best practice looks like for each type of campaign and routineEngineNew campaigns start from proven practice instead of a blank page
FoundationKnowledge about the company and its audiences: who we are, and the markets, verticals and accounts we sell toKnowledge about the company and its market must live in one place instead of being retold in every campaignCompanyMakes output genuine: the right voice, the right audiences, provable claims. Without Foundation the engine is generic
Campaign briefsThe decision for one initiativeEvery initiative requires choices neither the machine nor the rules can make: what we want to achieve, for whom, whenCompanyGives direction and makes the initiative measurable. The only place a deliberate deviation from the rules can be decided

Three rules govern how the layers interact:

  • Doctrines bind unconditionally. Every piece of content passes them, in every campaign. They state boundaries and principles, never fixed numbers. A single campaign may deviate, but only as an explicit, flagged decision in its brief. A doctrine covers one discipline; new research amends the existing doctrine for its discipline or adds a new one, never a second document on the same subject. Admission to a doctrine is gated by an editorial process owned by the engine owner.
  • Playbooks advise once. A playbook seeds a new campaign or routine at creation, then the brief and plan own everything. Playbooks must themselves comply with the doctrines.
  • Rules live as data, skills enforce them. A doctrine owns its rules; the skills that produce and review content bind to them by reference and never restate them. Learning from any installation graduates upward into the engine layers deliberately, in customer-neutral form; engine layers never push company knowledge downward into Foundation.
Paid

The paid track — the engine writes the spec, a human launches it

Paid runs the same Stage 1→4 spine as everything else, with one deliberate difference at the end: nothing goes live on the engine’s authority. The engine builds the asset kit, validates it against the channel’s own format spec and writes the launch spec plus a paid approval item in the operator’s queue — the operator launches. Four channels are reachable — LinkedIn, Google, Meta and Microsoft — through one shared executor form, so a new paid channel is a stamp, not a project. A live campaign spends money against a targeting choice, so the engine does all the work up to the button and stops there, on purpose.

ABM

The account track — from one qualified company to several people inside it

Broad campaigns talk to a market; the account track talks to one company at a time — the unit of work is an account, not a market, and accounts belong to industries. A qualified account gets a workspace under its industry, a playbook written on top of the industry layer, and a time-sequenced multi-stakeholder plan whose touches run through the normal Stage 2→3 spine: same voice gate, same queue, same delivery gating. Existing customers are a surface, not an exclusion: the motion on the account card decides the approach — a deployed customer is an expansion conversation owned by sales, and nothing in the track opens a cold pitch to a company that is already a customer.

Roles

Roles and authority — campaigns and routines are free, the Foundation is guarded

The engine is operated by named operators — the roster IS foundation/company/persona-voices/ (one file = one operator) — with two authority roles layered on top.

Operator — works the queue: reviews, approves, comments; may create, activate and feed campaigns and routines.
Engine owner — owns the guarded core: Foundation builds, settings/gtm-config.yaml and host machine + scheduling.
Campaign coordinator — owns the portfolio and its cross-campaign ceilings, judges campaign collisions, never the content.

Campaigns and routines are free — anyone with a persona file can open and run them; what stays guarded is the Subject’s knowledge (Foundation), the engine’s wiring (settings) and where it runs (host/scheduling).

The folder you are in IS the Subject. Each Industry and each Account is a self-contained workspace. Stakeholders are leaf files, not folders.
